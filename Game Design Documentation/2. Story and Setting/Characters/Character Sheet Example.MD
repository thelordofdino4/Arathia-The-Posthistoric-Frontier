# Character Template

Use this sheet to keep a character's choices, voice, relationships, and limits consistent across Arathia's stories and game material.

## How to use this template

- Start with the quick reference. A minor or early-stage character may need only that and a source or open-question note. When copying this template, keep the prompts you need and replace the guidance with your answers.
- Expand the detailed sections that help the next scene or design task. Keep answers brief, and link to longer histories, scene plans, or technical documents.
- Remove unused prompts and optional extensions. Leave a meaningful gap marked **Open** rather than inventing an age, scar, family, feeling, ability, or future event to complete the sheet.
- Keep a detail in one main place. The quick reference summarizes it; other sections can link back to it.
- Identify the story period this sheet describes. For a character used across eras, record important changes without mixing their earlier and later states.

Use these labels where the status of a detail could be misunderstood:

- **Established:** A current author decision or accepted source detail. Cite the decision or source.
- **Provisional:** The chosen working version, still awaiting a decision or check.
- **Interpretation:** An inference from material, with its basis stated.
- **Open:** Unknown or unresolved. Keep unused possibilities separate from the working version.

## 1. Quick reference

Aim for a few lines that are useful while writing a scene.

- **Name and identity:**  
  *Current name; in-world aliases if any; species or artificial nature. Add pronouns, gender, age, activation date, or lineage when established and relevant. Separate earlier development names from in-world identities.*
- **Current context:**  
  *Story period, location, occupation or operational role, and affiliation.*
- **Character premise:**  
  *Who are they, and what makes their presence consequential or distinctive in this story?*
- **Current aim and approach:**  
  *What do they want now, how do they pursue it, and what stands in their way? What is at stake for them or others?*
- **Response under pressure:**  
  *One characteristic choice and the main boundary or constraint shaping it.*
- **Recognizable presence:**  
  *A useful voice, action, sensory, or visual cue. Use an observable detail.*
- **Reference status:**  
  *Last reviewed; key source or decision link; the most important unresolved point for the next writing pass.*

## 2. Motivations and choices

- **Driving values and beliefs:**  
  *What do they value, assume, or believe about themselves and others? Distinguish their belief from what the story establishes as true.*
- **Goals and stakes:**  
  *Define the immediate objective and any relevant longer-term aspiration. What can they gain or lose, and who else bears the consequences?*
- **Methods and opposition:**  
  *What actions do they take to pursue the goal? Who or what resists them, and how do they respond when the first approach fails?*
- **Decision rules and boundaries:**  
  *What do they prioritize when values compete? What will they refuse, permit, or sacrifice? A self-imposed boundary belongs here; a limit on what they can actually do belongs in section 5.*
- **Behavioral strengths and flaws:**  
  *Describe habits of judgment or conduct that help and hinder them. Show when the same tendency becomes useful or costly. Keep technical expertise in section 5.*
- **Choice under pressure:**  
  *Give one sourced or clearly provisional situation: pressure, choice, reason, cost, and effect on others. Include doubt, attachment, or an internal conflict only when supported or deliberately chosen.*

## 3. Presence and voice

- **Appearance or embodiment:**  
  *Relevant build, movement, attire, markings, modifications, housing, interfaces, or distributed presence. Describe how these appear here; their functional effects belong in section 5. A visible body or humanoid avatar is optional.*
- **Communication and observable habits:**  
  *Vocabulary, rhythm, formality, silence, nonverbal signals, or other communication channels. What changes with the audience or under stress? Connect personality descriptions to things the reader or player can observe.*
- **Voice or behavior example:**  
  *A brief sourced line or action, or a clearly provisional writing example. Show how they answer a challenge, make a request, or conceal a motive. Record a catchphrase only if one is established or intentionally being developed.*

## 4. Active relationships

Repeat this entry for relationships that affect the current story. It can describe a person, AGI, group, or institution.

- **Counterpart and present dynamic:**  
  *Who is involved? What does each side want from the other? Identify relevant trust, dependence, authority, leverage, cooperation, or friction. How does a consequential choice affect the relationship? Link to the counterpart's reference when available.*

Formative relationships belong in section 6. If the same relationship remains active, describe its present dynamic here and link to its history.

## 5. Capabilities and practical limits

- **Abilities and means:**  
  *Relevant learned skills, innate abilities, technical knowledge, social or institutional authority, weapons, tools, artifacts, and enhancements. State what these allow the character to do. Ordinary expertise needs no special-power label.*
- **Costs and constraints:**  
  *Physical, cognitive, resource, environmental, legal, or operational limits. Include dependencies, access permissions, preparation, recovery, and trade-offs when consequential. Identify which limits are fixed and which can change.*
- **Knowledge boundary:**  
  *What do they know, misunderstand, or lack access to at this point? How did they learn it? The author's knowledge of world history or future events does not give the character that knowledge.*
- **Failure and counteraction:**  
  *What can interrupt, expose, defeat, or redirect their approach? How could another character detect or challenge it? Keep the mechanism consistent with the abilities and access above.*

## 6. Relevant history

- **Origin and formation:**  
  *Upbringing, creation, activation, migration, or another relevant origin. Include only circumstances that help explain the current character.*
- **Formative events and ties:**  
  *Select events and relationships that shaped a present belief, habit, goal, or capability. Explain that connection. Include family, creators, mentors, peers, and communities where applicable.*
- **Learning and training:**  
  *How did they acquire important skills or knowledge? Education, experience, cultural learning, AI mentorship, or technical development may apply. Link to a longer chronology if needed.*

## 7. Narrative use and direction

- **Story function:**  
  *What does this character cause, reveal, obstruct, or make possible? A role such as antagonist, mentor, protagonist, witness, or supporting character can help, but should lead to a concrete contribution.*
- **Trajectory:**  
  *Choose change, steadfastness, deterioration, revelation, an unresolved direction, or another intended pattern. Specify what changes or holds steady under pressure. Separate change in the character from change in the reader's understanding. Growth, redemption, and remorse are optional.*
- **Consequences and future threads:**  
  *What follows from their choices, intended or unintended? Include personal, relationship, community, or wider-world effects at the appropriate scale. Label a possible later appearance or storyline as a possibility until adopted. Link to extended scene or plot planning.*

## 8. Sources and open decisions

- **Sources and current decisions:**  
  *Link to relevant scenes, lore, references, and dated author decisions. Put a source beside a claim when it would be difficult to verify from a general source list. Identify older drafts as older drafts.*
- **Conflicts and working version:**  
  *Where sources disagree, record the competing claims and which version is currently being used, if chosen. Cite the decision; keep unresolved differences visible.*
- **Next questions:**  
  *Prioritize the gaps that affect the next scene or design task. State the question and what it prevents you from writing confidently. Keep unadopted ideas clearly separate.*

## Optional extensions

Add only the extensions that serve this character and the current task.

### Biology culture and artificial architecture

Use this extension for consequential detail beyond the presence described in section 3 and the abilities or constraints in section 5. Extend those entries or link to technical references rather than repeating them.

- **Embodiment and dependencies:**  
  *Relevant senses, movement, environmental needs, energy, maintenance, lifespan, or lifecycle. Explain the effect on action or interaction.*
- **Social and cultural context:**  
  *Relevant community practices, language, status, obligations, or identity. Distinguish individual choices from assumptions about an entire species or culture.*
- **Artificial architecture where applicable:**  
  *Location or distribution, interfaces, authority, permissions, cross-checks, isolation, backups, copies, and continuity of identity. Establish only the details needed by the story; link to technical rules for the rest.*

### World connections themes and design references

- **Wider setting connection:**  
  *How do relevant institutions, factions, ecology, technology, or historical events affect the character's choices? What do they affect in return? Link to shared lore instead of repeating it.*
- **Themes and meaningful motifs:**  
  *What question or tension do their choices explore? Include visual symbolism, personal or cultural artifacts, flashback triggers, or memory cues when these have a useful role. Avoid assigning symbolism to every object or making every character represent a universal theme.*
- **Design references:**  
  *External inspirations or archetypes, if useful. Record the specific feature being drawn on and the choices that make this character distinct.*

### Game interaction and implementation

- **Player interaction:**  
  *What can the player ask, learn, obtain, refuse, or change through this character? Define their practical quest, companion, adversary, service, or other gameplay role where applicable.*
- **Behavior and state changes:**  
  *Relevant dialogue responses, routines, combat or noncombat behavior, relationship changes, and reactions to player choices. Show how these express the character's established goals and boundaries.*
- **Rules and implementation references:**  
  *Link to quest, dialogue, encounter, or systems documents. Add numbers, rewards, conditions, or branching outcomes only when selected for this design; mark proposals as provisional.*

## Check before using the sheet

- Can I explain the character's next meaningful choice, approach, opposition, and stakes?
- Do the voice and behavior examples distinguish them from another member of the cast?
- Have I separated behavioral flaws from practical limits, and past influences from current relationships?
- Can I tell which details are established, provisional, inferred, open, or unused possibilities?
- Does the sheet fit this character's nature and intended trajectory without requiring a body, family, humanlike emotion, or personal transformation?
- Are repeated details consolidated, optional material removed when unnecessary, and longer references linked?
- Do the period, knowledge, abilities, and consequences agree with the current story decisions?
